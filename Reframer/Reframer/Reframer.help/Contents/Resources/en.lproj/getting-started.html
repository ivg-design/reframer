Getting Started

Turn a local video into a reference overlay.

  1. Activate the empty-state Open action, drop a file, or press ⌘O.
  2. Choose a supported local file. To use YouTube, choose Open YouTube Video, paste a link, review the first-use notice, and complete the required Made for Kids preflight.
  3. Press Space to play or pause.
  4. Use ⌘ Page Down and ⌘ Page Up to step.
  5. The video and controls share one window. Drag the control-bar grip to move the complete overlay, including on the first click while Reframer is inactive. Focus the grip and use ⌥Arrow (add ⇧ for 10 points), or choose a named VoiceOver direction. macOS and window managers such as Mosaic move and resize the entire unlocked overlay. Drag the video itself to pan.
  6. Set opacity, size, and zoom. At 920 points and wider, controls use one 48-point row. Below 920 through the 640-point minimum, they use two rows totaling 96 points. Every control remains visible and accessibility-reachable.
  7. For local media, press L to place the complete overlay at the public status-bar tier above all ordinary app windows and make both video and controls click-through. Use ⌘⇧L from the app underneath to restore interaction.

Reframer checks that macOS can decode a usable video track before presenting the asset as ready.

VP8/VP9 WebM is prepared as temporary ProRes 4444/PCM so alpha is preserved. YouTube never autoplays, uses adaptive quality, and keeps its standard player UI and fullscreen behavior. YouTube stays unlocked so its controls, captions, settings, fullscreen, and links remain interactive; use Always on Top When Unlocked.

Locking requires the exact configured global Lock/Unlock chord to be registered. Reframer refuses lock without that recovery path and automatically unlocks with guidance if the registration later becomes unavailable.
